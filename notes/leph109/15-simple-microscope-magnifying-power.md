# Magnifying Power of a Simple Microscope

**NCERT sections covered:** 9.7.1


## Magnifying power of a simple microscope (NCERT 9.7.1)

A simple microscope is a single convex lens with the object **between O and F** (lecture 08). The image A′B′ is **virtual, erect and magnified**, on the same side as the object.

**Definition.** Let $\beta$ be the angle the final image subtends at the eye. Let $\alpha$ be the angle the object subtends when it is placed directly in front of the eye at the **least distance of distinct vision, $D$** (25 cm).

$$m = \frac{\beta}{\alpha}$$

Both angles are small, so $\tan\alpha \approx \alpha$ and $\tan\beta \approx \beta$.
- With the eye at the lens, the image subtends the same angle as the object at distance $u$, so $\tan\beta = AB/u$.
- Placed at $D$, $\tan\alpha = AB/D$.
- $u$ and $D$ are both measured against the light, so both are negative and the signs cancel:

$$m = \frac{\tan\beta}{\tan\alpha} = \frac{AB/u}{AB/D} = \frac{D}{u}$$

**In terms of $f$ and $v$.** From the lens formula $\frac1f = \frac1v - \frac1u$, put in the signs ($u$ and $v$ both negative, $f$ positive):
$$\frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f} + \frac{1}{v} \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad m = D\left(\frac{1}{f} + \frac{1}{v}\right)$$
Here $u$ and $v$ are now magnitudes.

**Case 1: final image at the least distance of distinct vision ($v = D$).**
$$m = D\left(\frac1f + \frac1D\right) = 1 + \frac{D}{f}$$

**Case 2: final image at infinity ($v = \infty$, normal adjustment).**
$$m = D\left(\frac1f + \frac1\infty\right) = \frac{D}{f}$$

The magnifying power is **smaller in normal adjustment**, yet this mode is preferred. With the image at infinity the eye is **relaxed**, while focusing on an image at $D$ strains it.

## Numerical

**Q.** A convex lens of focal length 5 cm is used as a magnifying glass. Find its magnifying power if the final image is formed (i) at $D$, (ii) at infinity.

(i) $m = 1 + \dfrac{D}{f} = 1 + \dfrac{25}{5} = 6$

(ii) $m = \dfrac{D}{f} = \dfrac{25}{5} = 5$
