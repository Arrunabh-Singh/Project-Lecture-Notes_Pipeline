# Important Rays for Drawing Mirror Ray Diagrams

**NCERT sections covered:** 9.2, 9.2.2


## Why special rays?

From a point **B** of an object, countless rays strike the mirror. For any one of them you *could* draw the normal through C and apply $\angle i = \angle r$. To avoid that work every time, four rays whose reflected path is already known are used. **Any two** of them fix the image: the image of a point is where two reflected rays intersect, or where they *appear* to intersect when produced backwards.

## The four important rays (NCERT 9.2.2)

**1. A ray parallel to the principal axis passes through the focus after reflection.**

- Concave mirror: the reflected ray actually passes through F.
- Convex mirror: the reflected ray **appears to diverge from F** behind the mirror (draw the backward extension dashed).

This is why a concave mirror is **converging** in nature (a parallel beam meets at F) and a convex mirror is **diverging** in nature (a parallel beam spreads as if from F).

**2. A ray passing through the focus becomes parallel to the principal axis after reflection.**

- Concave mirror: a ray through F reflects parallel.
- Convex mirror: a ray **directed towards F** (behind the mirror) reflects parallel to the axis.

**3. A ray passing through the centre of curvature retraces its path after reflection.**
Compare a plane mirror hit at normal incidence: $\angle i = 0$ and so $\angle r = 0$, and the ray comes straight back. A ray through C travels along a radius, so it meets the mirror *along the normal* and returns the same way. For a convex mirror, the ray **directed towards C** retraces its path.
*Quiz form:* if a figure shows a ray striking a mirror and retracing its path, the point it was aimed at is **C** (not F or P).

**4. A ray incident obliquely at the pole P is reflected obliquely with $\angle i = \angle r$.**
At P the principal axis is itself the normal (it passes through C), so the reflected ray makes the same angle with the axis on the other side. This is true for both concave and convex mirrors.
