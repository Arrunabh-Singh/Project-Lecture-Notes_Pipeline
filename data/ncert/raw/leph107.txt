Chapter Seven ALTERNATING CURRENT

7.1 INTRODUCTION
DC vs AC. Mains supply varies as sine function. AC preferred: transformers allow efficient voltage conversion, economical long-distance transmission.

7.2 AC VOLTAGE APPLIED TO A RESISTOR
v = vm sin ωt (7.1)
i = im sin ωt (7.2), im = vm/R (7.3) — Ohm's law, v and i in phase.
Instantaneous power p = im²R sin²ωt (7.4); average <sin²ωt> = 1/2
Average power p̄ = (1/2)im²R (7.5c)
rms current I = im/√2 = 0.707im (7.6)
Average power P = I²R (7.7)
rms voltage V = vm/√2 = 0.707vm (7.8)
V = IR (7.9)
Named: Nicola Tesla (rotating magnetic field, induction motor, polyphase ac, Tesla coil), George Westinghouse (AC proponent vs Edison's DC, founded Westinghouse Co.)
Household line voltage 220V is rms, peak vm=√2×220=311V.

7.3 REPRESENTATION BY ROTATING VECTORS — PHASORS
Phasor: vector rotating about origin with angular speed ω. Resistor: V and I phasors in phase (zero phase angle).

7.4 AC VOLTAGE APPLIED TO AN INDUCTOR
v – L di/dt = 0 (7.10)
i = im sin(ωt – π/2) (7.12), im = vm/XL (7.14)
XL = ωL (7.13) — inductive reactance, unit ohm (Ω)
Current LAGS voltage by π/2 (quarter cycle).
Average power over cycle = 0 (no dissipation in pure inductor).

7.5 AC VOLTAGE APPLIED TO A CAPACITOR
v = q/C (7.15)
i = im sin(ωt + π/2) (7.16), im = vm/XC (7.18)
XC = 1/ωC (7.17) — capacitive reactance
Current LEADS voltage by π/2.
Average power over cycle = 0.

7.6 AC VOLTAGE APPLIED TO SERIES LCR CIRCUIT
L di/dt + iR + q/C = v (7.20)
i = im sin(ωt+φ) (7.21)
im = vm/√(R² + (XC–XL)²) (7.25a)
Z = √(R² + (XC–XL)²) (7.26) — impedance
tan φ = (XC–XL)/R (7.27)
If XC>XL: φ positive, circuit capacitive, current leads.
If XC<XL: φ negative, circuit inductive, current lags.

7.6.2 Resonance
Resonant frequency: ω0 = 1/√(LC) (7.28), at resonance XC=XL, Z=R (minimum), im=vm/R (maximum)
Applications: radio/TV tuning circuits. Resonance requires BOTH L and C present (not RL or RC alone).

7.7 POWER IN AC CIRCUIT: THE POWER FACTOR
P = VI cos φ (7.30a) = I²Z cos φ (7.30b)
cos φ = power factor
Case (i) Resistive: φ=0, cosφ=1, max power dissipation
Case (ii) Pure L or C: φ=π/2, cosφ=0, "wattless current"
Case (iii) LCR series: power dissipated only in R
Case (iv) At resonance: cosφ=1, P=I²R (maximum)
Low power factor → large transmission power loss (I²R); improved via capacitor addition (neutralizing wattless component).

7.8 TRANSFORMERS
Mutual induction principle. Primary Np turns, secondary Ns turns.
εs = –Ns dφ/dt (7.31), εp = –Np dφ/dt (7.32)
vs/vp = Ns/Np (7.33)
ip/is = vs/vp = Ns/Np (7.35)
Step-up: Ns>Np, Vs>Vp, Is<Ip. Step-down: Ns<Np, Vs<Vp, Is>Ip.
Energy losses: (i) flux leakage (ii) winding resistance/I²R heating (iii) eddy currents — reduced by laminated core (iv) hysteresis loss.
Power transmission: step-up at generator (reduce current, cut I²R loss), step-down at substations, ~240V to consumers.

SUMMARY
1. Resistor: i=vm/R sinωt, in phase.
2. rms: I=im/√2, V=vm/√2, P=IV=I²R.
3. Inductor: XL=ωL, current lags π/2, avg power=0.
4. Capacitor: XC=1/ωC, current leads π/2, avg power=0.
5. Series RLC: Z=√(R²+(XC-XL)²), φ=tan⁻¹((XC-XL)/R), P=VIcosφ.
6. Pure L/C: cosφ=0, wattless current.
7. Phasor: rotating vector representation.
8. Transformer: Vs/Vp = Ns/Np, Is/Ip = Np/Ns.

POINTS TO PONDER
1. Quoted ac values are rms unless stated.
2. Power rating = average power rating.
3. Power consumed in ac circuit never negative.
4. AC ampere defined via Joule heating (not mutual force like DC ampere, since attractive force averages to zero for AC).
5. Adding voltages across elements: must account phase — VRC=√(VR²+VC²), not VR+VC.
6. Voltage/current in phasor diagrams are scalars represented as rotating vectors (not true vectors).
7. No power loss in pure L or C; only resistive element dissipates energy.
8. Resonance (XL=XC) requires both L and C.
9. Power factor measures closeness to max power expenditure.
10. Motor: electrical→mechanical. Generator: mechanical→electrical.
11. Step-up transformer: voltage↑, current↓ proportionally (energy conserved).

Key terms/glossary: alternating voltage/current, phasor, inductive reactance XL, capacitive reactance XC, impedance Z, resonant frequency ω0, quality factor Q = ω0L/R = 1/(ω0CR), power factor cosφ, wattless current, transformer, step-up/step-down, eddy currents, hysteresis loss, flux leakage.
