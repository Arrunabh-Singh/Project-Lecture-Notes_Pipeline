Physics Chapter Six ELECTROMAGNETIC INDUCTION

154

6.1 INTRODUCTION
Faraday (England) and Joseph Henry (USA), ~1830: electric currents induced in closed coils by changing magnetic fields. Phenomenon: electromagnetic induction.

6.2 THE EXPERIMENTS OF FARADAY AND HENRY
Experiment 6.1: bar magnet moved towards/away from coil C1 connected to galvanometer G — deflection only during relative motion; larger deflection for faster motion; reversed for opposite pole/direction.
Experiment 6.2: coil C2 (steady current, battery) replaces bar magnet, moved towards/away from C1 — same effect. Relative motion between coils matters.
Experiment 6.3: two coils C1, C2 held stationary; C1 connected to battery via key K, C2 to galvanometer. Momentary deflection when K pressed/released, none when current steady. Deflection increases with iron rod inserted.

6.3 MAGNETIC FLUX
ΦB = B.A = BA cos θ (6.1)
ΦB = ΣBi.dAi (6.2), SI unit weber (Wb) = T m²

6.4 FARADAY’S LAW OF INDUCTION
ε = –dΦB/dt (6.3)
ε = –N dΦB/dt (6.4) for N-turn coil
Named: Michael Faraday (1791-1867) — electromagnetic induction, laws of electrolysis, benzene, electric motor/generator/transformer.

6.5 LENZ’S LAW AND CONSERVATION OF ENERGY
Heinrich Friedrich Lenz (1804-1865): "The polarity of induced emf is such that it tends to produce a current which opposes the change in magnetic flux that produced it." Negative sign in Eq 6.3 represents this. Connected to conservation of energy (no perpetual motion).

6.6 MOTIONAL ELECTROMOTIVE FORCE
ΦB = Blx; ε = –dΦB/dt = Blv (6.5), "motional emf"
Via Lorentz force: W = qvBl, ε = W/q = Blv
For stationary conductor, changing B: F = q(E + v×B) = qE since v=0 — time-varying B generates E.

6.7 INDUCTANCE
NΦB ∝ I; SI unit of inductance: henry (H), dimension [M L² T⁻² A⁻²]
6.7.1 Mutual inductance: N1Φ1 = M12 I2 (6.7); M12 = μ0 n1 n2 π r1² l (6.9, coaxial solenoids); M12 = M21 = M (6.12, reciprocity theorem)
ε1 = –M dI2/dt
6.7.2 Self-inductance: NΦB = LI (6.13); ε = –L dI/dt (6.14)
Long solenoid: L = μ0 n² A l (6.15); with core μr: L = μr μ0 n² A l (6.16)
Energy stored: W = ½LI² (6.17); energy density uB = B²/2μ0 (6.18)

6.8 AC GENERATOR
Nicola Tesla — credited with development. ΦB = BA cos ωt; ε = NBAω sin ωt (6.19) = ε0 sin ωt (6.20) = ε0 sin 2πνt (6.21)
India: 50 Hz mains frequency; USA: 60 Hz.
Hydro-electric, thermal, nuclear generators (mechanical energy source varies).

SUMMARY
1. ΦB = B.A = BA cos θ
2. ε = –N dΦB/dt (Faraday's law)
3. Lenz's law: induced emf opposes flux change (energy conservation)
4. Motional emf: ε = Blv
5. Inductance = flux-linkage/current = NΦ/I
6. Mutual inductance: ε1 = –M12 dI2/dt, M12 = M21
7. Self-inductance: ε = –L dI/dt
8. Long solenoid: L = μr μ0 n² A l
9. AC generator: ε = NBA(2πν) sin(2πνt)

POINTS TO PONDER
1. Oersted/Ampere (moving charge→field) then Faraday/Henry ~1830 (moving magnet→current) — deep symmetry.
2. Induced currents oppose flux change per energy conservation (closed circuit); open circuit induces emf across ends.
3. Motional emf explainable via Lorentz force (moving charge) OR via time-varying B inducing E even for stationary charge — hints at relativity principle relevance to Faraday's law.
