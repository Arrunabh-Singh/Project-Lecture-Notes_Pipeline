# Images Formed by a Concave Mirror

**NCERT sections covered:** 9.2.3


## Image formation by a concave mirror

The object is brought in from infinity towards the mirror: infinity → beyond C → at C → between F and C → at F → between P and F. The image changes in each case.

**How to draw:** draw the mirror, then the principal axis through its centre, and mark P, F and C, with C at twice the distance of F. Use a scale and put arrows on the rays. Solid lines mean real rays; dashed lines mean rays produced backwards (virtual).
*Size tip:* where the image will be smaller (object beyond C), draw the object a bit large. Where the image will be bigger (object between F and C), draw it a bit small. For the object at C, draw the inverted, same-size image first and then fit the rays to it.

| Position of object | Position of image | Nature and size |
|---|---|---|
| At infinity | At F | Real, inverted, **highly diminished** (point-sized) |
| Beyond C | Between F and C | Real, inverted, **diminished** |
| At C | At C | Real, inverted, **same size** |
| Between F and C | Beyond C | Real, inverted, **magnified** |
| At F | At infinity | Real, inverted, **highly magnified** |
| Between P and F | Behind the mirror | **Virtual, erect, magnified** |

Notes on the cases:

- **At infinity:** the incident rays are parallel and converge at F.
- **At F:** the two reflected rays come out parallel, so the image is at infinity. This is the reverse of the first case: object at infinity gives image at F, and object at F gives image at infinity. For the second ray you can use the ray through C, or the oblique ray at P. For a **point object** at F the reflected beam is parallel.
- **Between P and F:** this is the only case with a **virtual** image. The reflected rays **diverge**, so they are produced backwards (dashed) and *appear* to meet behind the mirror at **A′B′**. When using the oblique ray at P, make the two angles look equal.

All the real images are inverted and the virtual one is erect. Move about in front of a concave mirror and you see every one of these images in turn.

## Uses of concave mirrors

- **Dentist's mirror** — the tooth sits between P and F, so the dentist sees an erect, magnified image.
- **Shaving and dressing-table mirrors** — your face is between P and F, so you see yourself erect and magnified.
- **Searchlights and torches** — a bulb at the **focus** of a concave reflector gives a parallel beam (the "object at F → image at infinity" case). A large area is lit over a long distance. The shiny aluminium-coated cup behind a torch bulb is this reflector.
