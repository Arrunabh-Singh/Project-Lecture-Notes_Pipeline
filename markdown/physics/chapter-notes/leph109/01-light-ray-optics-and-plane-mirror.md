# Light, Ray Optics and the Plane Mirror

**NCERT sections covered:** 9.1, 9.2


## Nature of light (NCERT 9.1)

Light lets us see the objects around us — in a dark room nothing is visible until the light is switched on.

Light has a **dual nature**:

| Wave nature | Particle nature |
|---|---|
| explains **interference** and **diffraction** | explains the **photoelectric effect** (Einstein, Nobel Prize) |
| wave theory — **Huygens** | particles of light are **photons**, *bundles of energy*; corpuscular idea — **Newton** |

*Board slip:* the board writes "(Maxwell)" under the particle column. The teacher says **Newton** in class, and that is correct. Newton proposed the corpuscular (particle) theory and Einstein's photoelectric work introduced photons. Maxwell's contribution was the *electromagnetic wave* theory of light, which belongs in the wave column.

## Ray optics (NCERT 9.1)

**Ray optics is a limiting case of wave optics.** Light *can* bend round a corner, but only when the obstacle is extremely small. NCERT gives the reason: the wavelength of light (400–750 nm) is tiny compared with everyday objects. So for ordinary objects light travels in a straight line — **rectilinear propagation of light** — and does not bend while it stays in one medium. A straight-line path is a **ray**, and a bundle of rays is a **beam**.

## Reflection, absorption, transmission

Light is a form of energy. When it falls on a surface, three things can happen. It can be **reflected**, **absorbed** by the material, or **transmitted** (it goes through to the other side). How much of each happens depends on the surface:

- **Mirror** — highly reflecting; absorption and transmission are negligible. Mirrors are coated with highly reflective metals such as **silver or aluminium**. The back (non-reflecting) side is drawn with slant hatching.
- **Glass slab** — reflection is negligible and transmission is maximum (this comes back under refraction).
- **Wall painted black** — most of the light is absorbed. A candle in a dark room with black walls gives poor illumination, but placed in front of a mirror it gives more.

## Laws of reflection (NCERT 9.2)

Draw the **normal** at the **point of incidence**.

- **Angle of incidence $\angle i$** — the angle made by the incident ray with the normal.
- **Angle of reflection $\angle r$** — the angle made by the reflected ray with the normal; the reflected ray stays in the same medium.

1. $\angle i = \angle r$.
2. The incident ray, the normal *at the point of incidence*, and the reflected ray all **lie in the same plane**.

The teacher explains the second law with the three coordinate planes (xy, yz, xz). If the incident ray and the normal lie in one plane, the reflected ray is in that same plane — it can never jump into another plane. NCERT adds that both laws hold at every point of *any* surface, plane or curved.

## Plane mirror — characteristics

1. **Size of image = size of object.**
2. The image is formed **on the other side of the mirror**, at the **same distance from the mirror** as the object. An object 20 cm in front gives an image 20 cm behind.
3. The image is **virtual and erect**. *Virtual* means it is formed by rays that only *appear* to come from a point.
4. The image is **laterally inverted**. Take *y* as vertical and *x* as horizontal. There is no inversion along *y* (your head does not go down), but left and right swap along *x*. The letter **P** or **B** comes out reversed.

## Locating an image — ray diagram

**To locate a point of the image you need at least two rays.** Any number of rays leave each point of the object. Two are enough, and drawing more only clutters the figure.

- Take two rays from **B**. Each one reflects with $\angle i = \angle r$ about its own normal. Produce the reflected rays **backwards** (dashed lines) — they meet at **B′** behind the mirror.
- Do the same from **A** to get **A′**, then join **A′B′**. Using the two end points takes care of every point in between.
- The dashed lines are only *apparent* paths — no light actually comes from behind the mirror, and there is no source there. That is why a plane-mirror image is **virtual**.

Working the same construction point by point for the letter **P** shows lateral inversion directly. Points close to the mirror stay close to it in the image.
